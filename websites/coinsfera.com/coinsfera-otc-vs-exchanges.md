<h2>Understanding the Traditional Crypto Exchange (CEX) Model</h2>

<p>A traditional cryptocurrency exchange operates on an <strong>order-book model</strong>. This is a public ledger of "buy" and "sell" orders. When you place a market order to buy 1 BTC, the exchange matches you with the lowest available sell orders on the book. For a small trade, this happens almost instantaneously at the current market price.</p>

<p>However, the order book is only as deep as the current liquidity. If a "whale" attempts to buy 500 BTC in a single market order, they will quickly exhaust the lowest sell orders and begin eating into higher and higher price levels. This phenomenon is known as <strong>price slippage</strong>, and it is the primary enemy of the high-volume trader.</p>

<h3>The Limitations of CEXs for Institutional Traders</h3>
<ul>
    <li><strong>Market Impact:</strong> Large orders signal the market. When a massive buy order appears, other traders and bots notice, often driving the price up before the order is even fully executed.</li>
    <li><strong>Execution Risk:</strong> In volatile markets, the price can shift significantly between the moment a large order is placed and the moment it is filled.</li>
    <li><strong>Public Exposure:</strong> While account details are private, the impact of the trade on the public order book is visible to everyone, potentially exposing a firm's strategy.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Deep Dive: Price Slippage and the "Wall" Problem</h2>

<p>Price slippage is the difference between the expected price of a trade and the price at which the trade is actually executed. In the context of high-volume trading in Turkey, slippage can result in losses of thousands of dollars per transaction.</p>

<h3>The Mechanics of Slippage on Exchanges</h3>
<p>Imagine a scenario where the current price of Bitcoin is $60,000. The order book has 10 BTC available at $60,000, 20 BTC at $60,100, and 50 BTC at $60,200. If you want to buy 80 BTC immediately via a market order, you will pay $60,000 for the first 10, $60,100 for the next 20, and $60,200 for the remaining 50. Your <strong>average entry price</strong> will be significantly higher than the spot price you saw on your screen.</p>

<h3>How Coinsfera Eliminates Slippage</h3>
<p>Coinsfera solves this through <strong>fixed-price execution</strong>. When you engage with the Coinsfera OTC desk, you agree upon a single price for the entire volume. If the desk agrees to sell you 100 BTC at $60,000, you pay exactly that price for every single coin. There is no climbing the order book, no "averaging up," and therefore, zero slippage.</p>
