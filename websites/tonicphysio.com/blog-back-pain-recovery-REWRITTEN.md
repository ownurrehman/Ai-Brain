## What Causes Back Pain?

The lower back carries most of your body weight. When something fails in the spine, muscles, or joints, pain follows.

### Common Causes of Back Pain in Milton

- **Herniated disc** — The soft center of a spinal disc pushes through its outer layer. This presses on nearby nerve roots.
- **Sciatica** — When the L5 or S1 nerve is compressed, pain shoots down the leg. Often caused by a disc issue. [Learn about sciatica treatment in Milton →](/physiotherapy-in-milton/sciatica-treatment/)
- **Facet joint dysfunction** — Small joints at the back of the spine get stiff or inflamed. This limits movement and causes compensatory muscle strain.
- **Muscle strain** — Overstretched or torn fibres in the lower back muscles. Common after lifting, sports, or long hours at a desk.
- **Poor posture** — Forward head posture and rounded shoulders overload the lumbar spine over time.

### When to See a Professional

See a [physiotherapist in Milton](/physiotherapy-in-milton/) if you have:

- Pain lasting more than 2 weeks
- Numbness or tingling in your legs
- Weakness when standing or walking
- Pain that wakes you at night
- A recent injury or accident

## Acute vs. Chronic Back Pain

Your recovery plan depends on how long you have had pain and what is driving it.

### Acute Back Pain (0–6 Weeks)

- Starts after a specific event: lifting, a fall, sports injury, or awkward movement
- Usually sharp and localised
- Best approach: relative rest + gentle movement + early [physiotherapy assessment](/physiotherapy-in-milton/)

> **Key point:** Total bed rest is outdated. Gentle movement keeps joints lubricated and prevents stiffness.

### Chronic Back Pain (12+ Weeks)

- Persists beyond normal healing time
- Often involves "central sensitisation" — the nervous system stays on high alert even after tissues heal
- MRI may show nothing, yet pain continues
- Best approach: movement re-education, desensitisation, and gradual loading

| Stage | Duration | Pain Type | Best Treatment Focus |
|-------|----------|-----------|---------------------|
| Acute | 0–6 weeks | Sharp, localised | Calm inflammation, gentle mobility |
| Sub-acute | 6–12 weeks | Dull, intermittent | Restore range of motion, light strengthening |
| Chronic | 12+ weeks | Persistent, variable | Nervous system retraining, progressive loading |

## The 4-Step Back Pain Recovery Path

At Tonic Physio, we follow a clinical pathway that matches treatment to your stage of recovery. We do not jump to strengthening if your joint is still locked. We do not stop at pain relief if your core is still weak.

### Step 1: Assessment and Decompression

- Identify the exact pain trigger through orthopaedic testing and movement screening
- Use manual techniques and specific positions to create space in the joint
- Reduce nerve pressure and calm the inflammatory response
- Goal: Make movement safe again

### Step 2: Mobilisation and Nerve Glide

- Restore joint glide through manual therapy
- Break down adhesions that limit range of motion
- Add nerve gliding exercises so the sciatic nerve moves freely without pinching
- Goal: Full, pain-free range of motion

### Step 3: Progressive Loading

- Start with isometric holds (muscle contracts without moving the joint)
- Progress to functional movements and resistance
- Target deep core muscles: multifidus and transverse abdominis
- These muscles act as a natural brace for your spine
- Goal: Rebuild strength that supports daily life

### Step 4: Functional Integration

- Tailor exercises to your actual life in Milton
- Desk worker? Add ergonomic cues and movement breaks every 30 minutes
- Parent or athlete? Build rotational strength and lifting mechanics
- Goal: Handle your environment without regressing

## Which Treatment Is Right for You?

Patients in Milton often ask whether to see a chiropractor, physiotherapist, or osteopath. The answer depends on what your pain needs.

| Treatment | Best For | What It Does | Limitation |
|-----------|----------|--------------|------------|
| **Chiropractic** | Quick joint restriction relief | High-velocity spinal adjustments | Does not fix muscle imbalances |
| **Physiotherapy** | Long-term movement correction | Full-body assessment, exercise rehab, manual therapy | Takes longer than a single adjustment |
| **Manual Osteopathy** | Disc issues, chronic nerve pain, whole-body balance | Gentle mobilisation, myofascial release, fluid balance | Often combined with physio for best results |
| **Massage Therapy** | Muscle tension, stress-related tightness | Releases hip and glute tension that pulls on the lower back | Not a standalone fix for joint or nerve issues |

## What to Expect: Recovery Timeline

Every body heals differently, but most back pain cases follow this general pattern.

## How to Stop Back Pain from Returning

Most people fall into the "Pain-Rest-Pain" cycle: they hurt, they stop moving, their muscles weaken, and the pain returns the moment they try to be active again.

### Break the Cycle

- **Move daily** — Even 10 minutes of walking helps
- **Practise spinal hygiene** — Rotate, flex, and extend your spine through full range every morning
- **Set up your desk** — Screen at eye level, feet flat, hips slightly above knees
- **Take breaks** — Stand or walk for 2 minutes every 30 minutes
- **Strengthen your hips and glutes** — A stiff hip forces the lower back to overwork

### When to Book a Maintenance Session

- Every 4–6 weeks if you have a history of recurring back pain
- Before returning to sport or heavy labour
- After any minor flare-up, even if it resolves quickly

## FAQ

### How do I know if my back pain is a disc or muscle problem?

Muscle strain feels like a dull ache or tightness, usually tender to touch. Disc issues often shoot pain down the leg (sciatica) with tingling or numbness. Only a professional assessment can confirm the cause.

### Can I exercise with back pain?

Yes — but the right type matters. In the acute phase, exercise means gentle movement to avoid stiffness. In recovery, it means targeted strengthening. Avoid heavy lifting and high-impact activity until cleared by your therapist.

### How many physiotherapy sessions will I need?

Acute strains often resolve in 4–6 sessions. Chronic disc issues or post-surgical recovery can take several months. Tonic Physio gives you a detailed plan after your first assessment so there are no surprises.

### Is physiotherapy covered by insurance in Ontario?

Most extended health insurance plans cover physiotherapy. We offer direct billing for many providers. OHIP does not typically cover clinic-based physiotherapy, but [WSIB](/wsib-care-programs/) and auto insurance (SABS) cover workplace and accident-related injuries.

### What is the fastest way to relieve sciatica pain?

The fastest relief comes from reducing pressure on the sciatic nerve through decompression positions, manual therapy, and nerve glides. [Sciatica treatment at Tonic Physio](/physiotherapy-in-milton/sciatica-treatment/) targets the root cause, not just the leg pain.

### Does manual osteopathy work for back pain?

Yes. Manual osteopathy uses gentle mobilisation and myofascial release to restore joint movement and fluid balance around the spine. It is especially effective for disc issues and chronic nerve pain where high-velocity adjustments are not appropriate.

### Is shockwave therapy painful?

It can feel intense during treatment because the pulses deliver high energy to deep tissue. Intensity is adjustable. Most patients find the long-term reduction in chronic pain far outweighs the temporary discomfort.
