# Rheumatoid Arthritis and Physiotherapy: Managing Pain and Preserving Mobility

Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic autoimmune condition that causes inflammation in the joints, leading to pain, stiffness, swelling, and progressive joint damage. Unlike osteoarthritis, which is caused by wear and tear, rheumatoid arthritis occurs when the immune system mistakenly attacks the lining of the joints. For patients in Milton dealing with this condition, physiotherapy is a critical part of managing symptoms, maintaining function, and slowing the progression of joint deformity.

## Understanding Rheumatoid Arthritis

Rheumatoid arthritis typically affects the small joints of the hands and feet first, though it can involve any joint in the body. Common symptoms include symmetrical joint pain, morning stiffness lasting more than an hour, swelling and warmth in affected joints, fatigue, and reduced range of motion. Over time, chronic inflammation can erode cartilage and bone, leading to visible deformity and disability.

Because rheumatoid arthritis is systemic, it can also affect the eyes, lungs, heart, and blood vessels. Managing the condition requires collaboration between rheumatologists, family physicians, and allied health professionals including physiotherapists.

## Why Physiotherapy Is Essential for Rheumatoid Arthritis

Physiotherapy cannot cure rheumatoid arthritis, but it plays an essential role in symptom management, function preservation, and quality of life. During flare-ups, physiotherapy techniques help reduce inflammation and protect joints. During remission periods, therapy builds strength, improves range of motion, and prevents deconditioning.

At Tonic Physio, our approach is patient-centered and adaptive. We adjust every session based on your current symptoms, energy levels, and disease activity.

## Joint Protection Strategies

One of the core principles of rheumatoid arthritis physiotherapy is joint protection. Damaged joints are vulnerable to further injury, and certain movements or positions can accelerate deformity.

Your physiotherapist will teach you how to distribute weight evenly across joints, avoid prolonged positions that stress affected joints, use assistive devices when appropriate, modify daily activities to reduce strain, and balance rest with activity to prevent overuse.

These strategies preserve joint integrity while allowing you to maintain independence in daily tasks.

## Range of Motion Exercises

Maintaining joint range of motion is essential for preventing stiffness and contracture. Gentle range of motion exercises are performed daily, ideally during the warm part of the day when joints are less stiff.

Exercises are tailored to the joints affected by your condition. Hand exercises may include finger bends, thumb circles, and tendon gliding movements. Wrist and elbow exercises maintain forearm rotation and extension. Shoulder and hip exercises preserve the ability to reach, dress, and walk comfortably. Ankle and toe exercises support safe walking and balance.

Your therapist at Tonic Physio demonstrates each movement and ensures you are performing it safely and effectively.

## Strengthening for Rheumatoid Arthritis

Muscle weakness is common in rheumatoid arthritis due to pain, reduced activity, and the inflammatory process itself. Strengthening exercises support affected joints by improving stability and shock absorption.

We focus on isometric exercises during flares, when joint movement is painful, and progress to dynamic resistance exercises during stable periods. Resistance bands, light weights, and bodyweight exercises are selected to match your current strength and comfort level.

Strengthening the muscles surrounding inflamed joints reduces mechanical stress and delays the progression of joint damage.

## Managing Fatigue

Fatigue is one of the most debilitating symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis, and it is not relieved by rest alone. Physiotherapy helps by teaching energy conservation techniques, pacing strategies, and graded activity programs.

We help you identify the activities that drain your energy most and find more efficient ways to complete them. Aerobic conditioning, even at low intensity, can improve cardiovascular fitness and reduce overall fatigue over time.

## Pain Management Techniques

In addition to medical management, physiotherapy offers several pain management strategies. Heat therapy reduces joint stiffness and muscle tension. Cold therapy decreases swelling and numbs acute pain. TENS machines block pain signals through electrical stimulation. Gentle manual therapy improves circulation and reduces joint stiffness.

Aquatic therapy is particularly valuable for rheumatoid arthritis patients because warm water supports body weight, reduces joint load, and allows movement with less pain.

## Monitoring and Adjusting Treatment

Rheumatoid arthritis is variable. Symptoms fluctuate between flares and remissions, and treatment must adapt accordingly. At Tonic Physio, we maintain ongoing communication with your rheumatologist and adjust your physiotherapy program in response to changes in your condition, medication, or overall health.

## Assistive Devices and Adaptive Equipment

As the condition progresses, some patients benefit from splinting, bracing, or adaptive equipment. Custom hand splints protect vulnerable joints and maintain alignment during sleep or activity. Walking aids reduce lower extremity joint stress. Reachers and dressing aids maintain independence while protecting upper limb joints.

Our therapists assess your needs and fit appropriate devices based on your joint status and activity goals.
