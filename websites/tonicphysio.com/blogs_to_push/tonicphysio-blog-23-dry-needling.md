# Dry Needling at Tonic Physio: Relieving Muscle Pain with Precision

Dry needling is a highly effective treatment for muscle pain, tension, and movement dysfunction. It involves placing thin, sterile needles directly into trigger points and tight muscle bands to release pain, improve mobility, and promote healing. At Tonic Physio in Milton, our trained physiotherapists use dry needling as part of comprehensive treatment plans for back pain, neck pain, headaches, sports injuries, and chronic musculoskeletal conditions.

## What Is Dry Needling

Dry needling is a technique that targets myofascial trigger points, which are tight knots inside muscle tissue. These trigger points develop due to overuse, poor posture, injury, or stress. They cause localized pain and can refer pain to other areas of the body. For example, trigger points in the neck muscles often refer pain to the head, causing tension headaches.

The term dry needling distinguishes it from wet needling, which involves injecting medication. Dry needling uses the needle itself to create a mechanical and neurological response in the muscle. The needles are the same type used in acupuncture but the approach, assessment, and treatment goals are different.

## How Dry Needling Works

When a needle is inserted into a trigger point, it causes a localized twitch response. This twitch is a brief contraction followed by relaxation. It breaks the cycle of muscle spasm, increases blood flow to the area, and resets the nerve signals that keep the muscle tight.

The needle stimulus also prompts the body to release natural painkillers including endorphins, serotonin, and other neuromodulators. It activates the nervous system to modify pain perception and reduce hyperexcitability.

The result is often immediate relief of pain and tension, followed by continued improvement over the following twenty-four to forty-eight hours as tissue perfusion and mobility increase.

## Conditions Treated with Dry Needling

At Tonic Physio, dry needling is used for a wide range of conditions.

Chronic neck and upper back tension from desk work responds quickly when trigger points in the trapezius and levator scapulae muscles are released. Tension headaches and cervicogenic headaches caused by neck muscle trigger points improve with targeted treatment to the suboccipital and referral zones. Lower back pain from spinal stress and muscle guarding reduces when the deep spinal stabilizers and gluteal muscles are treated.

Shoulder pain including rotator cuff strain, impingement, and frozen shoulder benefits from dry needling to the deltoid, rotator cuff muscles, and scapular stabilizers. Hip and gluteal pain often involves trigger points in the piriformis, gluteus medius, and deep hip rotators. Hamstring and calf strain recovery is accelerated by releasing tight bands that restrict lengthening.

Jaw pain and temporomandibular dysfunction improve when the masseter and temporalis muscles are treated. Sports injuries including muscle strain, overuse, and biomechanical overload respond to dry needling as part of a broad rehabilitation plan.

## What to Expect During a Dry Needling Session

Your session at Tonic Physio begins with a brief assessment to identify the painful areas and locate active trigger points. The skin is cleaned. A thin needle is inserted into the muscle. You may feel a dull ache, a brief twitch, or a sensation of spreading release. The needle remains in place for a few seconds to several minutes depending on the treatment approach. After needling, your therapist may follow up with stretching, manual therapy, or exercise instruction.

Soreness similar to post-exercise muscle ache is common for twenty-four to forty-eight hours after treatment. This usually resolves quickly and is followed by improved range of motion and reduced pain.

## Is Dry Needling Safe

Dry needling is very safe when performed by trained physiotherapists. At Tonic Physio, all practitioners have advanced certification in dry needling and follow strict sterile needle protocols. Mild side effects include temporary soreness, bruising, or fatigue. We screen all patients for bleeding disorders, needle phobia, local infection, and pregnancy-related precautions before treatment.

## Dry Needling and Your Overall Treatment Plan

Dry needling is not a standalone treatment. At Tonic Physio, it is integrated with exercise therapy, manual therapy, and movement retraining to produce lasting results. Needling addresses the acute muscle dysfunction, while active rehabilitation addresses the underlying causes to prevent recurrence.
