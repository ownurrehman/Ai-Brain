# Spinal Decompression Therapy: Reliving Pressure on Your Spine

Spinal decompression is a non-surgical treatment designed to relieve pressure on spinal discs and nerves. It is frequently used for patients with herniated discs, sciatica, spinal stenosis, and chronic back pain caused by disc bulges. At Tonic Physio in Milton, we offer spinal decompression as part of a comprehensive physiotherapy program that helps patients avoid surgery and return to normal activity.

## How Spinal Decompression Works

Spinal decompression uses controlled mechanical traction to gently stretch the spine. This creates negative pressure within the spinal discs, which can help retract bulging or herniated disc material away from sensitive nerve roots. The negative pressure also improves nutrient and oxygen flow into the disc space, promoting healing in chronically compressed spinal segments.

The treatment uses a motorized table that applies precise traction force to the lower back or neck depending on the area being treated. Therapy sessions last approximately thirty minutes and are performed in a relaxed, painless manner.

## Conditions Treated with Spinal Decompression

Spinal decompression is most effective for specific spinal conditions including lumbar disc herniation causing sciatica, degenerative disc disease with chronic back pain, spinal stenosis with radicular symptoms, foraminal narrowing compressing nerve roots, and facet joint syndrome aggravated by disc compression.

It is not appropriate for all back conditions. If you have severe osteoporosis, spinal fusion hardware, a history of vertebral fracture, or advanced spinal instability, your physiotherapist will recommend alternative treatments.

## What to Expect During Treatment

During your spinal decompression session at Tonic Physio, you lie comfortably on a decompression table. A harness wraps around your pelvis, and another secures your trunk or upper body. The table applies a controlled stretch that cycles through phases of tension and relaxation. Many patients find the sensation similar to a gentle traction massage. The computer-controlled system monitors resistance and adjusts force in real time for safety and effectiveness.

Sessions are conducted two to three times per week for four to six weeks depending on the diagnosis and response.

## Combining Decompression with Physiotherapy

Spinal decompression works best when combined with active rehabilitation. After the mechanical pressure is reduced, your surrounding muscles need strengthening to maintain spinal alignment and stability. At Tonic Physio, we pair decompression sessions with core stabilization exercises, manual therapy, postural coaching, and movement education to ensure lasting results.

Without active rehabilitation, spine instability can lead to recurrence once decompression stops.

## Research and Evidence

While spinal decompression has been used for decades, high quality clinical trials remain limited. Many patients report significant pain relief during and after treatment, but results are individualized. The best outcomes tend to occur in patients with confirmed disc herniations who comply with the full rehabilitation program that accompanies decompression.

We do not claim that decompression works for everyone, and we will tell you honestly during your assessment whether you are a good candidate.
