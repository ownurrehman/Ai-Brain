# Tennis Elbow and Golfer's Elbow: Physiotherapy for Tendon Pain

Tennis elbow and golfer's elbow are overuse conditions that affect the tendons connecting the forearm muscles to the elbow. Despite their names, they are not limited to tennis players or golfers. They affect anyone who performs repetitive gripping, lifting, or computer work. At Tonic Physio in Milton, we treat both conditions successfully with targeted physiotherapy that reduces pain, restores strength, and prevents recurrence.

## Understanding Tennis Elbow and Golfer's Elbow

Tennis elbow, also called lateral epicondylitis, is a degenerative condition of the tendons that attach to the outside of the elbow. These are the wrist extensor muscles. Pain is felt on the outside of the elbow and may radiate down the forearm. It is worsened by gripping, lifting, and twisting motions.

Golfer's elbow, or medial epicondylitis, affects the tendons on the inside of the elbow. These are the wrist flexor muscles. Pain is felt on the inner elbow and is particularly aggravated by throwing, golf swings, and forearm work.

Both conditions involve microtears and collagen degeneration at the tendon insertion. The condition is similar to plantar fasciitis in that chronicity is driven by poor blood supply and failed healing rather than active inflammation.

## Risk Factors

Common risk factors include repetitive gripping and wrist extension or flexion, computer work with poor ergonomics and excessive mouse use, manual labor involving tools, weightlifting with poor form, and sudden increases in repetitive activity. Age is also a factor, with most cases occurring between thirty and fifty years of age.

At Tonic Physio, our assessment identifies the specific activities and biomechanical factors that contribute to your condition.

## The Tonic Physio Elbow Assessment

Your assessment includes a detailed history covering symptom duration, aggravating activities, and previous treatments. We test elbow and wrist range of motion. We palpate the elbow tendons to confirm the diagnosis. We assess grip strength with dynamometry. We evaluate cervical spine and nerve tension since nerve irritation can mimic or exacerbate elbow pain. We assess your workstation, sport technique, or activity biomechanics. We identify any coexisting shoulder or wrist conditions that influence elbow loading.

## Physiotherapy Treatment for Elbow Tendinopathy

Treatment at Tonic Physio is progressive and evidence-based.

Manual therapy includes soft tissue release for the forearm muscles, joint mobilization, and nerve gliding if nerve involvement is suspected. Taping provides temporary relief and supports the tendon during daily activity. Exercise therapy is the foundation of recovery. Eccentric loading exercises rebuild tendon capacity and stimulate collagen alignment. Wrist and forearm strengthening addresses the muscles that control grip and elbow load.

Activity modification is essential. We identify the movements that caused the overload and teach you how to modify or avoid them during recovery. Ergonomic assessment and correction prevent recurrence for computer workers and manual laborers.

For chronic cases that do not respond to exercise alone, we use shockwave therapy. The acoustic pulses stimulate tissue regeneration in degenerated tendon structures. We also consider custom bracing for work-related strain when a temporary reduction in load is necessary.

## Recovery Timeline

Acute cases respond in four to eight weeks. Chronic cases lasting months may require eight to twelve weeks. Recurrence is common unless contributing activities and biomechanics are addressed. At Tonic Physio, we build relapse prevention into every treatment plan.
