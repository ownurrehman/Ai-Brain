# Physiotherapy for Sciatica: Targeted Treatment for Nerve Pain

Sciatica is one of the most common and debilitating conditions treated at Tonic Physio in Milton. It causes sharp, shooting pain that travels from the lower back through the buttock and down the leg, sometimes reaching the foot. For many patients, the pain is so severe that walking, sitting, and sleeping become impossible. Fortunately, physiotherapy provides highly effective treatment for most cases of sciatica without surgery or injections.

## What Is Sciatica

Sciatica is not a diagnosis but a symptom. It describes irritation or compression of the sciatic nerve, the largest nerve in the body. The sciatic nerve is formed from nerve roots exiting the lower spine and travels through the pelvis and down the back of each leg. Irritation can occur anywhere along this pathway.

The most common cause of sciatica is a herniated disc in the lumbar spine pressing on the nerve root as it exits the spinal canal. Other causes include spinal stenosis narrowing the space where the nerve travels, piriformis syndrome compressing the sciatic nerve as it passes through the buttock muscles, degenerative disc disease reducing disc height and increasing nerve tension, spondylolisthesis shifting a vertebra forward, and spinal tumors which are rare but serious.

## Symptoms of Sciatica

Sciatica symptoms vary depending on where and how severely the nerve is irritated. Most patients experience pain that radiates from the lower back or buttock down the back or side of the leg. Numbness, tingling, or burning sensations often accompany the pain. Muscle weakness in the leg or foot may develop in severe cases. Pain is usually worse with sitting, coughing, sneezing, or forward bending. Some patients find relief when walking or changing positions.

## The Tonic Physio Assessment for Sciatica

Your assessment at Tonic Physio begins with a complete history of your symptoms. We ask when the pain started, what activities worsen or relieve it, and whether you have had previous episodes. We assess your spinal posture, range of motion, and movement patterns. We use specific tests to determine whether your symptoms originate from the spine, hip, or piriformis muscle. Neurological testing checks sensation, reflexes, and muscle strength in the affected leg. We also assess the lumbar spine and hip joints to identify contributing factors.

If your symptoms indicate a more serious condition, we refer you directly for medical imaging or specialist consultation.

## Physiotherapy Treatment for Sciatica

Treatment for sciatica at Tonic Physio is personalized based on the cause, severity, and irritability of your symptoms.

Manual therapy including spinal mobilization, soft tissue release, and nerve gliding techniques reduces compressive forces and restores mobility. Exercise therapy forms the core of long-term management. Directional preference exercises, often based on the McKenzie Method, centralize leg pain back to the spine. Core stabilization exercises support the lumbar spine and reduce recurrence. Nerve mobilization techniques gently stretch and slide the sciatic nerve to improve its movement and reduce sensitivity.

Pain management strategies include heat and ice application, TENS for symptom relief, and education on posture and positioning. Activity modification is crucial during the acute phase. We help you identify movements and positions that aggravate your symptoms and replace them with safe alternatives.

## When Surgery Is Considered

Most sciatica cases resolve with conservative physiotherapy within six to twelve weeks. Surgery is considered only when there is progressive neurological deficit such as worsening weakness or numbness, loss of bowel or bladder control, or severe pain that does not improve after a full course of physiotherapy. At Tonic Physio, we monitor your neurological status at each session and communicate with your physician if surgery becomes necessary.

## Recovery Timeline for Sciatica

Acute sciatica often begins improving within two to four weeks of consistent treatment. Some relief is often felt within the first few sessions. Chronic sciatica may require a longer commitment to rehabilitation. Recurrence is prevented by addressing the underlying mechanical causes and maintaining the strength and mobility gained during treatment.
