## What Is the Pelvic Floor

The pelvic floor is a group of muscles that sit at the base of the pelvis like a hammock. These muscles support the bladder, bowel, and reproductive organs. They control continence, assist with sexual function, and provide stability to the spine and pelvis during movement. When these muscles become weak, tight, or uncoordinated, dysfunction occurs.

## What Happens During a Pelvic Floor Assessment

A pelvic floor assessment at Tonic Physio is professional, respectful, and confidential. The therapist begins with a detailed history of your symptoms, medical background, and lifestyle factors. For an internal examination, the therapist uses a gloved finger to assess muscle tone, strength, coordination, and any areas of tenderness. This is done only with your full consent and only if it is appropriate for your condition. The therapist also assesses your breathing, core activation, posture, and movement patterns since these all influence pelvic floor function. If an internal examination is not appropriate or comfortable, external assessment and treatment can still be highly effective.

## Treatment Techniques in Pelvic Floor Physiotherapy

Pelvic floor physiotherapy treatment at Tonic Physio is personalized and gentle.

Pelvic floor muscle training involves teaching proper contraction and relaxation of the pelvic muscles. Many patients have been doing Kegels incorrectly for years, which can worsen symptoms if the muscles are already tight. Internal manual therapy releases trigger points and muscle tension within the pelvic floor when indicated. Biofeedback training uses sensors or visual cues to help you learn correct muscle activation patterns. Education on bladder and bowel habits reduces urgency, frequency, and leakage. Core and hip strengthening addresses the support structures that work with the pelvic floor. Down-training and relaxation are critical when muscles are overactive and in spasm. Scar tissue mobilization helps after delivery or surgery.

## Why Pelvic Floor Physiotherapy Is Not Just Kegels

Many patients are told to simply "do Kegels," but this advice can be harmful. If your pelvic floor is tight or overactive, doing more contractions will increase pain and dysfunction. A proper assessment determines whether your muscles need strengthening, lengthening, or coordination training. At Tonic Physio, we never prescribe exercises without first assessing what the muscles actually need.

## Pregnancy and Postpartum Pelvic Health

Pregnancy places significant strain on the pelvic floor, abdominal wall, and pelvic joints. Postpartum recovery often requires more than time and rest. Our therapists assess diastasis recti, pelvic floor trauma, cesarean section healing, and pelvic girdle pain. We help new mothers return to exercise safely and prevent long-term complications.
