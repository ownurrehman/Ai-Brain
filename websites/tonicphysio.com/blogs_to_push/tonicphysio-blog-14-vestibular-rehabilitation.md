# Vestibular Rehabilitation: Regaining Balance and Confidence

The vestibular system is a complex network in your inner ear and brain that controls balance, spatial orientation, and eye movements. When this system is disrupted by injury, illness, or degeneration, the result can be persistent dizziness, vertigo, unsteadiness, and nausea. At Tonic Physio in Milton, our vestibular rehabilitation program uses targeted exercises and manual techniques to retrain this system and restore normal balance function.

## Causes of Vestibular Dysfunction

Vestibular problems can develop for many reasons. Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo occurs when calcium crystals in the inner ear become dislodged and move into sensitive canals. Vestibular neuritis and labyrinthitis are viral infections that inflame the vestibular nerve or inner ear structures. Meniere disease involves excess fluid in the inner ear causing episodic vertigo, hearing loss, and tinnitus. Acoustic neuroma is a benign tumor on the vestibular nerve that grows slowly and disrupts balance. Head injury including concussion can damage the vestibular system directly or through whiplash forces on the neck and inner ear. Age-related degeneration affects vestibular function in older adults, contributing to falls and imbalance.

Post-surgical complications, stroke, ototoxic medications, and autoimmune conditions can also cause vestibular dysfunction.

## Symptoms That Indicate Vestibular Rehabilitation

You may benefit from vestibular rehabilitation if you suffer from spinning sensation or vertigo with specific head movements, persistent dizziness not explained by other causes, unsteadiness or difficulty walking straight, nausea triggered by visual motion, difficulty focusing your eyes during head movement, frequent falls, or chronic imbalance that limits your activity.

Many of these symptoms are dismissed by patients as normal aging or unrelated issues. In fact, most vestibular conditions are treatable with physiotherapy once properly diagnosed.

## The Vestibular Rehabilitation Assessment

At your first visit to Tonic Physio, our trained vestibular therapist conducts a thorough assessment. We review your medical history, onset of symptoms, and previous diagnoses. We check eye movements using specialized tests that detect abnormal nystagmus and smooth pursuit dysfunction. We evaluate head and eye coordination with gaze stabilization and eye-head movement testing. We assess balance and postural control using standardized outcome measures. We examine your neck for cervical contributions to dizziness. We also test functional tasks such as walking and turning to understand how your symptoms affect real-world activity.

## Core Vestibular Rehabilitation Techniques

Treatment is customized based on the cause and severity of your condition.

Canalith repositioning maneuvers such as the Epley and Semont maneuvers are used to relocate displaced crystals in benign paroxysmal positional vertigo. These are performed in the clinic, and most patients experience immediate and lasting relief after one or two sessions.

Gaze stabilization exercises improve your ability to keep your eyes focused on a target while your head moves. This is essential for reading, driving, and walking in busy environments.

Habituation exercises gradually expose your brain to movements that trigger dizziness, reducing symptom sensitivity over time. This technique is particularly effective for vestibular neuritis and chronic unilateral vestibular loss.

Balance retraining challenges your postural system using varied surfaces, visual conditions, and dual-task activities. This restores stability and reduces fall risk.

Cervical spine manual therapy addresses neck dysfunction that contributes to cervicogenic dizziness. Many patients have both vestibular and cervical causes for their symptoms.

## Expected Timeline for Recovery

Recovery from vestibular dysfunction varies by cause. Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo often resolves in one to three sessions. Vestibular neuritis typically requires six to twelve weeks of progressive exercise for the brain to compensate for the damaged input. Bilateral vestibular loss may require ongoing maintenance exercises. Chronic imbalance and Meniere disease respond gradually and require periodic follow-up to adjust the program as symptoms fluctuate.
