# Ankle Sprain Physiotherapy: Recovering Fully and Preventing Recurrence

Ankle sprains are among the most common orthopedic injuries. They occur when the ligaments that stabilize the ankle are overstretched or torn, usually during a sudden twist or roll of the foot. While many people assume a sprained ankle will heal on its own, research shows that without proper rehabilitation, ankle sprains lead to chronic instability, repeated injury, and early arthritis. ## Types of Ankle Sprains

Ankle sprains are classified by severity and location.

Grade one sprains involve mild stretching of the ligament without tearing. They cause mild pain and swelling with minimal loss of function. Grade two sprains involve partial tearing of the ligament. They cause moderate pain, swelling, and bruising with difficulty bearing weight. Grade three sprains involve complete rupture of the ligament. They cause severe pain, significant swelling, and an inability to bear weight.

Lateral sprains affect the outside ligaments and are the most common, usually occurring when the ankle rolls inward. Medial sprains affect the inside ligament and are less common but often more serious. High ankle sprains involve the ligaments above the ankle joint and take significantly longer to heal.

At Tonic Physio, we assess the severity and type of your sprain to guide the right treatment approach.

## The Acute Phase: Protecting the Injury

In the first few days after an ankle sprain, protection is critical.

At Tonic Physio, we guide you through immediate management using relative rest, ice, and elevation to control swelling. Early gentle movement is encouraged to prevent stiffness, but weight-bearing is progressed based on pain levels. For severe sprains, temporary bracing or taping may be recommended to protect healing tissue.

We discourage prolonged immobilization, which leads to muscle atrophy, joint stiffness, and longer recovery times.

## Restoring Range of Motion and Strength

Once swelling begins to subside, rehabilitation focuses on restoring movement and building strength.

Ankle range of motion exercises restore normal flexion and extension. Calf and toe strengthening addresses muscles that support the ankle during walking and running. Resistance band exercises target the specific muscles that control ankle inversion and eversion. Balance exercises rebuild proprioception, which is the body's awareness of joint position.

Proprioception is particularly important after ankle sprains because ligament damage reduces the ankle's ability to sense its position, leading to repeated rolling and re-injury.

## Balance and Proprioception Training

Balance training is the most important predictor of whether an ankle sprain will recur.

At Tonic Physio, we progress balance exercises from standing on stable ground on one foot, to unstable surfaces like foam pads, to dynamic movements like single-leg hops and sport-specific drills. This progressive challenge forces the nervous system to relearn how to control the ankle and prevents chronic instability.

## Return to Sport and Activity

Returning to sport, running, or demanding work requires that the ankle meets specific functional benchmarks. These include full pain-free range of motion, strength within ten percent of the uninjured side, successful completion of balance and hopping tests, normalized gait without a limp, and confidence in cutting, jumping, and changing directions.

At Tonic Physio, we test these parameters and provide graduated return protocols. Returning before meeting these benchmarks significantly increases the risk of re-injury.

## Chronic Ankle Instability

Some patients develop chronic ankle instability after repeated sprains. The ligaments remain loose, proprioception is impaired, and the ankle gives way frequently. Physiotherapy is still effective but requires a longer program focused on balance, neuromuscular control, and sometimes bracing. If conservative management fails, surgical consultation may be necessary.
