# Physiotherapy Office Ergonomics: Setting Up Your Desk for Pain Prevention

If you work at a desk, you likely spend many hours in the same position every day. Over time, poor posture, screen positioning, and keyboard placement create chronic tension in the neck, shoulders, and lower back. The good news is that most desk-related pain is preventable with proper workstation setup. At Tonic Physio in Milton, our physiotherapists regularly treat office workers whose pain could have been avoided with simple ergonomic adjustments.

## Why Desk Work Causes Pain

The human body is made to move. Sitting for long periods causes several problems.

Muscles shorten and tighten around the hips, chest, and hamstrings. The spine rounds forward, placing strain on the discs, ligaments, and joints of the neck and lower back. The muscles that support the shoulders and shoulder blades switch off from disuse, while other muscles compensate and become overworked. Blood circulation slows, reducing nutrient delivery and waste removal from tissues. Nerve compression can occur at the wrist, elbow, or cervical spine due to sustained positions.

At Tonic Physio, we treat these conditions every day. The most effective solution is often prevention through proper desk ergonomics.

## Monitor Position

Your monitor should be positioned directly in front of you at arm's length distance. The top of the screen should be at or slightly below eye level. This prevents the forward head posture that causes neck strain and tension headaches.

If you use a laptop, elevate the screen using a stand or stack of books and add an external keyboard and mouse. Looking down at a laptop screen for hours every day is a major cause of neck and shoulder problems.

## Keyboard and Mouse Placement

Your keyboard should be positioned so your elbows are at ninety degrees and your wrists remain in a neutral position. Your forearms should rest lightly on the desk surface. The mouse should be placed directly beside the keyboard at the same level. You should not have to reach forward to use it.

Wrist rests are not recommended unless used only during pauses in typing. Resting your wrist while typing creates sustained pressure on the median nerve and is associated with wrist pain and repetitive strain injuries.

## Chair Setup

Your chair should support the natural curve of your lower back. The seat height should be adjusted so your feet rest flat on the floor with your knees at approximately ninety degrees. If your feet do not reach the floor, use a footrest.

Armrests should support your elbows without lifting your shoulders. If the armrests are too high, they force your shoulders into an elevated position that leads to upper back and neck tension.

## Sitting vs Standing Desks

Standing desks have become popular in office environments. While standing reduces some risks of prolonged sitting, it is not a complete solution. Standing for long periods can cause foot, knee, and lower back fatigue. Alternating between sitting and standing is the best approach.

At Tonic Physio, we recommend changing position every thirty to sixty minutes regardless of whether you are sitting or standing.

## Microbreaks and Movement

No ergonomic setup can completely offset the effects of staying in one position for hours. Schedule microbreaks every thirty to forty-five minutes. Stand, walk, and stretch briefly. Movement restores circulation, activates stabilizing muscles, and resets joint position.

Simple desk exercises include scapular retraction squeezes, chin tucks to restore neck alignment, gentle chest stretches, standing back extensions, and calf raises.

## When to See a Physiotherapist

If desk work has already caused pain or discomfort, professional assessment is recommended. Pain that persists for more than two weeks, numbness or tingling in the arms or hands, recurring headaches, lower back pain with sitting, and shoulder tension that does not improve with self-care are signs that physiotherapy can help.
