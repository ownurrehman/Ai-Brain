## What Is Acupuncture

Acupuncture is a therapeutic technique that originated in traditional Chinese medicine and is now widely practiced in modern healthcare settings. It involves inserting very fine, sterile needles into specific points on the body. These points lie along pathways called meridians, which traditional theory describes as channels of energy flow. Contemporary research explains acupuncture effects through neuroscience, showing that needle stimulation activates nerve pathways, releases endorphins, reduces inflammation, and modulates pain signals in the brain and spinal cord.

At Tonic Physio, our practitioners combine traditional point selection with modern anatomical knowledge to treat chronic pain conditions effectively.

## How Acupuncture Works for Chronic Pain

Chronic pain persists long after an injury has healed because the nervous system becomes oversensitized. Pain signals continue firing even when there is no new tissue damage. Acupuncture addresses this through several mechanisms.

Endorphin release occurs when needles stimulate nerve fibers, triggering the brain to produce natural painkillers. These chemicals reduce pain intensity and create a sense of relaxation. Nerve modulation happens when acupuncture changes how the spinal cord processes pain signals, effectively turning down the volume on chronic pain pathways. Muscle relaxation follows needle insertion in tight bands of muscle, known as trigger points, allowing spasms to release and restoring normal movement patterns. Improved circulation results from local vasodilation around needle sites, bringing oxygen and nutrients to damaged tissues while clearing inflammatory waste products.

## Types of Chronic Pain Acupuncture Can Treat

Our acupuncturists at Tonic Physio treat a wide range of chronic pain conditions.

Chronic lower back pain is one of the most common reasons patients seek acupuncture. Persistent lumbar pain that has not responded to medication often improves significantly with a course of needle therapy. Neck pain and headaches including tension headaches, cervicogenic headaches, and chronic migraines respond well to acupuncture that targets both local neck muscles and distal points that influence pain pathways. Osteoarthritis pain in knees, hips, and hands is increasingly treated with acupuncture as a safer alternative to daily anti-inflammatory medication. Fibromyalgia, a condition characterized by widespread muscle pain and fatigue, often shows improvement with regular acupuncture combined with gentle movement therapy.

Other conditions we treat include sciatica, shoulder pain, tennis elbow, plantar fasciitis, and pain following surgery or injury.

## What Happens During an Acupuncture Session

Your first acupuncture session at Tonic Physio begins with a thorough assessment. Your practitioner reviews your medical history, current medications, pain patterns, and previous treatments. We identify the areas where you experience pain and assess which acupuncture points are most likely to help.

You lie comfortably on a treatment table while the practitioner inserts thin, sterile, single-use needles. Most patients feel minimal discomfort. Some describe a mild tingling, warmth, or heaviness around the needle site. This is normal and indicates that the point is activating. Needles remain in place for fifteen to thirty minutes while you rest in a quiet, dim room. Many patients find this deeply relaxing and may even fall asleep.

After the session, you can return to normal activities immediately. Some patients feel immediate relief. Others notice gradual improvement over the next twenty-four to forty-eight hours.

## How Many Sessions Are Needed

The number of acupuncture sessions needed for chronic pain varies based on how long you have had the condition and how your body responds to treatment.

Acute pain may resolve in two to four sessions. Chronic pain that has lasted for months or years typically requires six to twelve sessions before significant improvement is achieved. Severe or complex conditions may need ongoing maintenance sessions every few weeks to sustain results.

At Tonic Physio, we create individualized treatment plans and reassess your progress regularly. If acupuncture is not producing the expected results after four sessions, we adjust the approach or integrate other therapies.

## Is Acupuncture Safe

When performed by a registered practitioner using sterile, single-use needles, acupuncture is extremely safe. Side effects are rare and usually mild. The most common effects include slight bruising at the needle site, brief lightheadedness after the first session, temporary fatigue, or a short-term increase in symptoms before improvement begins.

Our acupuncturists at Tonic Physio follow strict infection control protocols. We screen every patient for contraindications such as bleeding disorders, pacemakers, or pregnancy before beginning treatment.

## Acupuncture and Physiotherapy Together

Acupuncture works best when combined with other treatment approaches. At Tonic Physio, we often integrate acupuncture into broader physiotherapy programs. Needle therapy reduces pain enough that manual therapy and exercise become tolerable. Exercise then rebuilds strength and function to prevent pain from returning.

Your therapist may alternate sessions, providing acupuncture in some visits and active rehabilitation in others. This combined approach addresses both the immediate symptoms and the underlying causes of chronic pain.

## What to Expect from Your First Appointment

Before your first acupuncture session at Tonic Physio, eat a light meal and avoid alcohol or excessive caffeine. Wear loose, comfortable clothing that allows access to your legs, arms, and back. Bring a list of your current medications and supplements.

During your appointment, be open about your symptoms and any concerns. If you are uncomfortable with a particular technique or needle location, tell your practitioner immediately. Communication ensures the safest and most effective treatment experience.
