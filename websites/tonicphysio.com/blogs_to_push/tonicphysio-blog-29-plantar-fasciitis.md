# Plantar Fasciitis Physiotherapy: Relief for Heel Pain

Plantar fasciitis is the most common cause of heel pain in adults. It causes sharp stabbing pain at the bottom of the heel, usually worst in the first few steps of the morning. For many people, the condition persists for months or even years despite rest, stretching, and over-the-counter supports. At Tonic Physio in Milton, our physiotherapy program for plantar fasciitis combines manual therapy, exercise, and orthotic support to resolve heel pain permanently.

## Understanding Plantar Fasciitis

The plantar fascia is a thick band of connective tissue that runs from the heel bone to the base of the toes. It supports the arch of the foot and absorbs shock during walking and running. Plantar fasciitis occurs when this tissue becomes irritated and degenerated at its attachment to the heel bone.

Despite the name ending in itis, the condition is not primarily inflammatory. It is a degenerative process involving microtears, collagen breakdown, and poor blood supply. This explains why anti-inflammatory medications often provide only temporary relief.

## Risk Factors for Plantar Fasciitis

Certain factors increase the likelihood of developing plantar fasciitis. Flat feet or high arches alter the stress on the plantar fascia. Tight calf muscles and limited ankle flexibility increase tension on the heel. Obesity places excess load on the foot. Prolonged standing or walking on hard surfaces contributes to tissue breakdown. Sudden increase in running mileage or intensity overloads the plantar fascia. Poor footwear with inadequate arch support or excessive wear allows abnormal strain.

Our assessment at Tonic Physio identifies which of these factors are active in your case.

Your assessment includes a detailed history of symptom duration, activities, and previous treatments. We examine your foot posture, arch height, and heel alignment. ## Physiotherapy Treatment for Plantar Fasciitis

Treatment at Tonic Physio addresses all contributing factors.

Manual therapy includes calf release, ankle mobilization, and plantar fascia-specific soft tissue techniques. Tight calves are a primary cause of excessive heel stress, and releasing them is central to treatment. Stretching exercises target the calf, plantar fascia, and toe flexors.

Strengthening exercises focus on the foot intrinsic muscles that support the arch, as well as the hips and core to improve overall limb alignment. Night splints may be recommended to maintain gentle calf stretch during sleep. Taping provides temporary arch support and pain relief during recovery.

Custom orthotics are prescribed when foot posture contributes heavily to the condition. Shockwave therapy is used for chronic cases that have not responded to standard care. The acoustic waves stimulate tissue regeneration in degenerated fascia.

Exercise progressions move from basic stretching and strengthening to functional tasks like walking, standing, running, and sport-specific activity.

## Recovery Timeline

Most patients notice some improvement within two to four weeks of consistent treatment. Significant relief typically occurs within six to twelve weeks. Chronic cases lasting more than twelve months may require additional time or advanced interventions. Once resolved, maintenance exercises and proper footwear are crucial to prevent recurrence.
