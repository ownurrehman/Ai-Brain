# Hip Pain Physiotherapy: Femoroacetabular Impingement, Labral Tears, and Arthritis

Hip pain affects people of all ages, from young athletes dealing with femoroacetabular impingement to older adults managing osteoarthritis. The hip is a ball-and-socket joint that carries significant body weight and must move efficiently in multiple directions. When hip function is compromised, the result can be groin pain, stiffness, difficulty walking, and compensatory back and knee problems. At Tonic Physio in Milton, our hip physiotherapy program addresses the complex anatomy and biomechanics of the hip to restore pain-free movement.

## Common Causes of Hip Pain

Hip pain has multiple origins, and accurate diagnosis is essential for effective treatment.

Femoroacetabular impingement occurs when extra bone growth around the hip socket or femoral head pinches the labrum and cartilage during movement. It causes sharp groin pain with sitting, squatting, and twisting. Hip labral tears involve the cartilage ring that lines the socket. They cause pain, clicking, and a sense of locking or catching in the joint. Hip osteoarthritis is age-related wear of the cartilage and joint surfaces, causing stiffness and a grinding sensation with movement. Greater trochanteric pain syndrome causes pain over the outside of the hip where the gluteal muscles attach to the femur. It is often called bursitis though the primary cause is usually tendinopathy.

Hamstring and adductor strains create groin and posterior thigh pain that limits running and kicking. Iliopsoas tendinopathy causes deep groin pain and difficulty lifting the thigh. Hip flexor tightness from prolonged sitting creates front hip stiffness and abnormal gait.

At Tonic Physio, we differentiate these conditions through detailed clinical testing rather than relying solely on imaging.

## The Tonic Physio Hip Assessment

Your hip assessment at Tonic Physio includes a complete history of symptom onset, activities, and previous injuries. We observe your standing posture and walking pattern. We test hip range of motion in flexion, extension, rotation, and abduction. We perform special tests to distinguish joint, labral, and muscular pathology. We assess strength of the deep hip rotators, gluteals, hip flexors, and core stabilizers. We evaluate mobility of the lumbar spine, pelvis, and knees since these areas compensate for hip dysfunction.

## Physiotherapy Treatment for Hip Pain

Treatment at Tonic Physio is structured and progressive.

For femoroacetabular impingement, we use activity modification to avoid aggravating positions while progressively restoring motion and strength. Labral tears are treated with hip stability exercises, core strengthening, and movement retraining to reduce shear forces on the labrum. Osteoarthritis responds well to joint mobilization, strengthening, weight management education, and gait training. Greater trochanteric pain syndrome improves with gluteal strengthening, tendon loading progressions, and manual therapy.

For muscle strains, we use graded loading to rebuild tissue capacity while protecting healing fibers. Iliopsoas and hip flexor conditions improve with lengthening exercises, core control, and movement education. Postural correction and activity modification are included for all presentations.

## Surgery vs Conservative Management for Hip Conditions

Many hip conditions improve with physiotherapy alone. Labral tears and femoroacetabular impingement are often managed successfully without surgery unless there is significant joint damage or failed conservative care. Osteoarthritis requires ongoing management but rarely needs replacement in its early stages.

At Tonic Physio, we communicate with orthopedic surgeons in Milton and surrounding areas to coordinate care when surgery is necessary. Post-operative rehabilitation follows specific protocols based on the procedure performed.

## Expected Recovery Timelines

Greater trochanteric pain syndrome generally improves in six to twelve weeks with proper loading. Hip flexor and adductor strains take four to eight weeks depending on severity. Labral tears managed conservatively take eight to sixteen weeks. Femoroacetabular impingement requires three to six months of consistent rehabilitation for lasting improvement. Post-arthroscopy hip rehabilitation takes four to six months for full return to sport.
