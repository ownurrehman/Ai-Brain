<h2>What Is Back Pain?</h2>

<p>Back pain is discomfort anywhere along the spine, from the neck to the tailbone. Most cases involve the lumbar region, the five vertebrae between the ribs and pelvis.</p>

<h3>Why the Lower Back Fails</h3>

<p>The lumbar spine carries most of your body weight. It must stay stable enough to support your torso yet mobile enough to bend and twist. This constant demand makes it the most common site of injury.</p>

<p>When any part of this system fails, pain signals fire. The key is identifying which structure is failing.</p>

<h3>Common Causes of Back Pain</h3>

<ul>
<li><strong>Herniated disc</strong> — The soft center pushes through the outer ring, pressing on nearby nerves</li>
<li><strong>Sciatica</strong> — Compression of the L5 or S1 nerve root shoots pain down the leg, often from a disc issue</li>
<li><strong>Facet joint dysfunction</strong> — Small guiding joints at the back of the spine get stiff or inflamed</li>
<li><strong>Muscle strain</strong> — Overstretched fibres in the erector spinae or quadratus lumborum</li>
<li><strong>Poor posture</strong> — Forward head posture and rounded shoulders overload the lumbar spine over time</li>
</ul>

<h2>Acute vs Chronic Back Pain</h2>

<p>Your treatment plan depends on how long you have had pain and what is driving it.</p>

<h3>Acute Back Pain (0-6 Weeks)</h3>

<p>Acute pain starts after a specific event. This could be lifting a heavy box, a fall, or an awkward movement during sport.</p>

<p>The pain is usually sharp and localised. The goal in this phase is to calm the nervous system and reduce inflammation.</p>

<p>Total bed rest is outdated. Gentle movement keeps joints lubricated and prevents stiffness from setting in. Early <a href="https://tonicphysio.com/physiotherapy-in-milton/">physiotherapy assessment</a> prevents acute pain from becoming chronic.</p>

<h3>Chronic Back Pain (12+ Weeks)</h3>

<p>Chronic pain persists beyond normal healing time. In many cases, the original tissue injury has healed, but the nervous system stays on high alert.</p>

<p>This is called central sensitisation. The brain and spinal cord become hyper-aware of pain signals. An MRI may show nothing, yet the patient still hurts.</p>

<p>Treatment shifts to movement re-education and desensitisation. The goal is to convince the brain that movement is safe again.</p>

<h2>The 4-Step Back Pain Recovery Path</h2>

<p>At Tonic Physio, we follow a clinical pathway that matches treatment to your stage of recovery. We do not jump to strengthening if your joint is still locked. We do not stop at pain relief if your core is still weak.</p>

<h3>Step 1: Assessment and Decompression</h3>

<p>We start by identifying the exact pain trigger. This includes orthopaedic testing and movement screening.</p>

<p>If a patient has a disc bulge, we use specific positions and manual techniques to create space in the joint. This reduces nerve pressure and calms the inflammatory response.</p>

<p>The goal is simple: make movement feel safe again.</p>

<h3>Step 2: Mobilisation and Nerve Glide</h3>

<p>Once acute pain is managed, we restore joint movement. Manual therapy breaks down adhesions that limit range of motion.</p>

<p>We add nerve gliding exercises. Think of the sciatic nerve as a string in a tube. If the tube is tight, movement hurts. Nerve gliding ensures the nerve slides freely.</p>

<p>The goal is full, pain-free range of motion.</p>

<h3>Step 3: Progressive Loading</h3>

<p>You cannot protect a back by keeping it still. The goal is to introduce safe loads.</p>

<p>We start with isometric holds. The muscle contracts without moving the joint. We then progress to functional movements and resistance.</p>

<p>The targets are the deep core muscles: multifidus and transverse abdominis. These muscles act as a natural brace for your spine.</p>

<p>The goal is rebuild strength that supports daily life.</p>

<h3>Step 4: Functional Integration</h3>

<p>The final stage tailors exercises to your actual life in Milton.</p>

<p>Desk worker? We add ergonomic cues and movement breaks every 30 minutes. Parent or athlete? We build rotational strength and proper lifting mechanics.</p>

<p>The goal is handle your environment without regressing into pain.</p>

<h2>What to Expect: Recovery Timeline</h2>

<p>Every body heals differently. Most back pain cases follow a general pattern. Knowing this helps patients stay committed during slower phases.</p>

<h3>Weeks 1-2: Pain Control</h3>

<p>The focus is reducing the threat signal. You should notice sharper pain fading and sleep improving.</p>

<p>Treatment includes 2-3 gentle movements, manual decompression, and possible <a href="https://tonicphysio.com/registered-massage-therapy/">massage therapy</a> to calm muscle guarding.</p>

<h3>Weeks 3-6: Mobility Restoration</h3>

<p>You will notice increased range of motion. Sitting and walking become easier.</p>

<p>Treatment shifts to joint mobilisation, nerve glides, and light strengthening. This is where most patients turn the corner.</p>

<h3>Months 2-4: Strength and Stability</h3>

<p>You return to 90% of normal activities. The focus is building a resilient back that handles stress.</p>

<p>Treatment includes progressive loading, core strengthening, and sport-specific or work-specific drills.</p>

<h3>Long Term: Maintenance</h3>

<p>The goal is keeping imbalances from returning. Most patients need a monthly check-in or a dedicated home routine.</p>

<p>People with recurring back pain benefit from maintenance sessions every 4-6 weeks.</p>

<h2>How to Stop Back Pain from Returning</h2>

<p>Most people fall into the Pain-Rest-Pain cycle. They hurt, they stop moving, their muscles weaken, and the pain returns the moment they try to be active again.</p>

<h3>Break the Cycle</h3>

<ul>
<li><strong>Move daily</strong> — Even 10 minutes of walking helps</li>
<li><strong>Practise spinal hygiene</strong> — Rotate, flex, and extend your spine through full range every morning</li>
<li><strong>Set up your desk</strong> — Screen at eye level, feet flat, hips slightly above knees</li>
<li><strong>Take breaks</strong> — Stand or walk for 2 minutes every 30 minutes</li>
<li><strong>Strengthen your hips and glutes</strong> — A stiff hip forces the lower back to overwork</li>
</ul>
