## 5. FAQ Section
**Q: What is the difference between Manual Osteopathy and Chiropractic care?**
A: While both focus on the musculoskeletal system, Manual Osteopathy uses a more holistic, gentle approach focusing on the integration of all body systems, whereas chiropractic care often emphasizes spinal alignment through high-velocity adjustments.

**Q: Is Manual Osteopathy safe for all ages?**
A: Yes, because the techniques are gentle and adaptable, Manual Osteopathy is safe for infants, seniors, and everyone in between.

**Q: How many sessions will I need to see results?**
A: Results vary by individual, but many patients feel immediate relief. For chronic conditions, a series of sessions is typically recommended to achieve lasting stability.

**Q: Do I need a doctor's referral for osteopathy in Milton?**
A: No, you can book an appointment directly with Tonic Physio without a referral.

**Q: Will the treatment be painful?**
A: Manual Osteopathy is designed to be gentle. While some deep tissue work can be intense, the process is focused on relief and should not be agonizing.

**Q: Can osteopathy help with migraines and headaches?**
A: Yes, by releasing tension in the neck, shoulders, and upper back, Manual Osteopathy often significantly reduces the frequency and intensity of tension headaches.

**Q: How does osteopathy help with digestion?**
A: Osteopaths use gentle visceral manipulation to improve the mobility of internal organs, which can support better digestive function and reduce bloating.

**Q: What should I wear to my appointment?**
A: We recommend comfortable, loose-fitting clothing that allows the practitioner to easily access the areas being treated.

**Q: Can I combine osteopathy with physiotherapy?**
A: Absolutely. Integrating Manual Osteopathy with physiotherapy provides a comprehensive approach to both structural alignment and functional strength.

**Q: How often should I come in for maintenance?**
A: Depending on your lifestyle and health goals, a monthly maintenance session is often sufficient to prevent the return of chronic tension.
