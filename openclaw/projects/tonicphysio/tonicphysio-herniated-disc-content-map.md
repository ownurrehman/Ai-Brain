**faq_heading**: Herniated Disc Treatment FAQs

**faq_q1**: What is a herniated disc?
**faq_a1**: It happens when the soft center of a spinal disc pushes through a crack in the tougher exterior, often pressing on nearby nerves.

**faq_q2**: Do I need surgery for a herniated disc?
**faq_a2**: Not always. Many people find significant relief through physiotherapy and conservative care before considering surgery.

**faq_q3**: How long does it take to heal?
**faq_a3**: Every person is different, but most see a noticeable improvement in a few weeks of consistent therapy.

**faq_q4**: Can I exercise with a herniated disc?
**faq_a4**: Yes, but it must be the right kind of exercise. We guide you on what to do and what to avoid.

**faq_q5**: Will physiotherapy help with sciatica caused by a disc?
**faq_a5**: Yes, we focus on reducing the nerve compression that causes the radiating pain of sciatica.

**faq_q6**: Is it safe to get manual therapy for a slipped disc?
**faq_a6**: When done by a trained professional, manual therapy is a safe and effective way to reduce pain and improve motion.

**faq_q7**: What is the difference between a bulging disc and a herniated disc?
**faq_a7**: A bulge is when the disc sags; a herniation is when the inner material actually leaks out. Both can cause pain.

**faq_q8**: Can stress make my disc pain worse?
**faq_a8**: Stress often leads to muscle tension, which can put more pressure on your spine and increase discomfort.

**faq_q9**: How often should I come for treatment?
**faq_a9**: Usually, once or twice a week depending on the severity of your injury and how you respond to therapy.
