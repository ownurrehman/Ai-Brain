## 7) Factual claims in the current article that need verification or rewriting

These are the lines most likely to need evidence, qualification, or a rewrite.

1. **“Aim to post something new at least once a week.”**
   - Too absolute. Frequency depends on competition, resources, and quality.

2. **“Change the publish date if you’ve made significant updates, as this can help bring those pages back into the spotlight.”**
   - Needs careful wording. Republishing dates alone do not improve rankings unless the content itself materially improves and signals freshness appropriately.

3. **“Use tools like Google’s disavow tool to remove any links from spammy or irrelevant websites.”**
   - Inaccurate as written. The disavow tool asks Google to ignore links; it does not remove them from the web.

4. **Implication that adding the current year to titles inherently helps.**
   - Can improve CTR when the content is genuinely current, but can also look manipulative if unsupported.

5. **General claim that regular updates automatically improve rankings.**
   - Needs nuance. Updates help when they improve relevance, accuracy, completeness, or intent match.

6. **Keyword placement language is oversimplified.**
   - “Search engines won’t know where to rank you” is too blunt; search engines use many signals beyond keyword placement.

7. **Structured data explanation needs qualification.**
   - Schema can help search engines understand a page and may support rich results, but it does not guarantee enhanced SERP features.
