## What Is Shockwave Therapy

Shockwave therapy, also known as extracorporeal shockwave therapy, uses acoustic pressure waves to deliver mechanical energy to injured tissue. These high-energy pulses stimulate cellular repair, increase blood flow, break down calcifications, and reduce pain signals. It is widely used for chronic tendon conditions, plantar fasciitis, calcific tendinopathy, and other stubborn soft tissue injuries.

At Tonic Physio, we use advanced shockwave technology that delivers focused or radial waves depending on the depth and location of the injury.

## Conditions Treated with Shockwave Therapy

Shockwave therapy is most effective for chronic conditions that have not responded to first-line treatment.

Plantar fasciitis causes heel pain that is often worst in the morning. After six to twelve weeks of stretching and strengthening, many patients still have symptoms. Shockwave therapy addresses the degenerative tissue changes and promotes regeneration. Tennis elbow or lateral epicondylitis involves the outer elbow tendons. Chronic cases frequently fail to resolve with rest and exercises alone. Shockwave stimulates tendon remodeling and reduces grip pain.

Calcific tendinopathy of the shoulder occurs when calcium deposits form in the rotator cuff. These deposits cause sharp pain and limited range of motion. Shockwave breaks down calcium and triggers the body to reabsorb it. Achilles tendinopathy and patellar tendinopathy in runners and jumpers respond well when conservative care has stalled. Proximal hamstring tendinopathy and greater trochanteric pain syndrome are gluteal and hip tendon conditions that improve with shockwave as part of a complete rehabilitation plan.

## How Shockwave Therapy Works

During treatment, a gel is applied to the skin over the painful area. The shockwave applicator is then positioned and activated, sending pressure waves into the tissue. The treatment takes approximately fifteen minutes. Most patients feel a deep tapping or pulsing sensation. Intensity is adjusted based on comfort level.

The mechanical energy from shockwave therapy triggers several biological responses. New blood vessel formation improves circulation to chronically underperfused tendon tissue. Collagen production increases, supporting structural repair. Pain signal modulation occurs at the nerve level, reducing the perception of chronic pain. Cellular metabolic activity rises, accelerating the removal of degenerative material and the formation of healthy tissue.

## What to Expect During Treatment

At Tonic Physio, your shockwave therapy appointment includes assessment to confirm the diagnosis and determine whether shockwave is appropriate. Treatment is applied directly to the affected area. You may experience mild soreness for twenty-four to forty-eight hours after the session. This is normal and indicates that the healing response has been triggered. Your physiotherapist will give you post-treatment instructions and exercises to support recovery.

## How Many Sessions Are Needed

Most patients require three to six sessions spaced one week apart. Improvement often begins after the second session, with full benefits developing over six to twelve weeks as the body remodels the tissue. Some patients experience immediate relief, while others notice gradual improvement over time.

## Shockwave and Rehabilitation Together

Shockwave therapy is most effective when combined with an active rehabilitation program. The treatment stimulates tissue repair, but muscles and joints still need strengthening, stretching, and movement retraining to prevent recurrence. At Tonic Physio, every shockwave patient receives a customized exercise program that supports the biological effects of treatment.

## Is Shockwave Therapy Safe

Shockwave therapy is clinically safe and well tolerated. It is non-invasive and does not involve medication or injections. Side effects are minimal and typically limited to temporary redness, bruising, or soreness at the treatment site. It is not appropriate during pregnancy, over areas with active infection or malignancy, over growth plates in children, or in patients with certain medical implants. Your therapist will screen you for these conditions before beginning treatment.
