# Whiplash Physiotherapy: Recovery After Neck Trauma

Whiplash is a neck injury caused by a sudden forward and backward movement of the head. It most commonly occurs during motor vehicle accidents when a vehicle is struck from behind, but it can also result from sports collisions, falls, and other impact events. At Tonic Physio in Milton, our whiplash physiotherapy program addresses the complex combination of muscle, joint, ligament, and neurological structures that are injured during these events.

## What Happens During a Whiplash Injury

During a typical rear-end collision, the head is thrown backward into extension and then forward into flexion. This rapid motion causes multiple injuries simultaneously.

Ligaments and facet joints in the cervical spine are overstretched and irritated. Muscles of the neck, upper back, and shoulders go into protective spasm. Intervertebral discs may suffer microtears or herniation. Nerve roots exiting the cervical spine can become irritated or compressed. The vestibular system in the inner ear may be disrupted, causing dizziness. The temporomandibular joint may be strained, leading to jaw pain and headaches.

Because multiple systems are affected simultaneously, whiplash treatment must be comprehensive and address each component.

## Symptoms of Whiplash

The most common symptoms include neck pain and stiffness, shoulder and upper back pain, reduced neck range of motion, headaches originating at the base of the skull, dizziness and vertigo, jaw pain, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, sleep disturbance, and arm pain or numbness if nerve roots are involved.

Symptoms may appear immediately after the accident or develop gradually over the following hours or days.

## Physiotherapy Treatment for Whiplash

Treatment at Tonic Physio is multi-modal and addresses all affected systems.

Manual therapy including spinal mobilization and soft tissue release reduces pain and restores neck mobility. Dry needling addresses muscle spasm and trigger points when appropriate. Exercise therapy begins early with gentle range of motion exercises and progresses to strengthening of the deep neck flexors and scapular stabilizers. Postural education reduces strain during daily activities. Vestibular rehabilitation is provided when dizziness or balance problems persist. Temporomandibular treatments address jaw pain and clicking. Gradual return to activity programming ensures that you resume work, exercise, and driving safely.

## The Importance of Early Treatment

Early physiotherapy leads to faster recovery and reduces the risk of chronic symptoms. Research shows that patients who begin physiotherapy within the first few weeks after injury recover faster than those who wait. ## Recovery Timeline for Whiplash

Mild whiplash cases resolve in four to six weeks. Moderate cases require eight to twelve weeks. Severe cases with multiple system involvement may take three to six months. A small percentage of patients experience chronic symptoms requiring ongoing management. The most important predictor of full recovery is receiving appropriate treatment early and maintaining active rehabilitation throughout recovery.
